● Not from picocom, not yet. The image on the chip can only echo keys: there is nothing in it
  that can accept a definition. The interpreter has to be added to figforth.asm, assembled
  on the PC and flashed. Once ok appears, everything after that can be done from picocom.

  How fig-FORTH users started

  That is in fact how it was done in the fig-FORTH era:

  1. They bought a listing. The Forth Interest Group sold the Installation Manual (the model,
     written in Forth, screen by screen) and a printed assembly listing for each CPU (6502,
     8080, 6800, PDP-11 and others).
  2. They typed the listing into their own assembler. The whole kernel was in it, not only
     the CODE words. INTERPRET, QUIT, WORD, NUMBER, : and ; were there as lists of addresses,
     exactly like your stand-in ABORT (one dw per word).
  3. They rewrote only the machine-specific part. That was KEY, EMIT, ?TERMINAL, CR and the
     disc read/write for their own hardware. Your boards/ch32v006/board.asm is that part.
  4. They assembled, loaded and got ok. From then on they never went back to the assembler,
     except to fix the kernel.
  5. They built everything else in Forth. The editor came as Forth source in the manual. It
     was typed in at the terminal or onto disc screens and then loaded with LOAD.

  So you are at step 2, with the nucleus done and the colon definitions still to write.

  What to add, in order

  Each stage can be tested in the emulator and then on the board.

  1. Get to ok. This needs the user variables, the small stack and arithmetic words, number
     output (<# # #S #> D. .), EXPECT, QUERY, WORD, -FIND, NUMBER, ERROR, INTERPRET, QUIT and
     the real ABORT. After this, 1 2 + . prints 3 ok and an unknown word prints ?.
  2. The compiler. This needs CREATE, ,, :, ;, [, ], LITERAL, IMMEDIATE, IF/ELSE/ENDIF,
     BEGIN/UNTIL/AGAIN, DO/LOOP, <BUILDS/DOES>, VLIST and FORGET. Now you define words from
     picocom.
  3. The disc. This needs BLOCK, BUFFER, UPDATE, FLUSH, LIST and LOAD. With it the editor can
     be loaded from screens, as it was then.

  Your README's "Next" section already lists where the model cannot be copied as it stands:
  4-byte cells, alignment, and the kernel being in flash.

  Two things that differ from a 1979 machine

  - New words live in RAM. The dictionary grows in the 8 KiB of RAM, so what you define from
    picocom is gone at power-off. On the old machines it was the same; the source on disc was
    what survived.
  - You have no disc. The natural stand-in is the free flash: from 0x4000 up there is room
    for about 44 screens of 1 KiB. That needs a flash-writing routine in board.asm, and it is
    a decision for stage 3, not now.
